The Engineering Mindset: Opening the Box

Whether prying open a sealed smartphone enclosure with a heat gun to replace a battery, mapping complex signal chains in Logic Pro, or diagnosing why an enterprise inventory system desynchronizes under peak load: I refuse the black box.

I believe the best digital tools are built by people who understand both ends of the spectrum:

  • Under the hood (Systems Thinking): Relational databases, state machines, atomic reservation locks, and predictable data pipelines.
  • In the hands (Human Interface Craft): Zero-latency interactions, keyboard-first ergonomics, tactile gesture controls, and clean mental models that respect human attention.
What drives me: Most broken workflows aren't caused by careless users; they're caused by software that ignores human limits. Good design doesn't add noise—it creates quiet, predictable guardrails so people can get their work done with peace of mind.

How I Work: The Design-to-Dev Bridge

My true superpower lies at the intersection of Product Design and Systems Architecture: I design 100% production-realistic interfaces in Figma, built from the ground up using strict auto-layout, an 8pt spatial grid, design tokens, and exhaustive state matrices (hover, focus, error, disabled). Because every component respects real CSS layout principles and database constraints, developers can build immediately without guesswork, missing assets, or implementation friction.

I prefer testing real software over presenting static slide decks. When tackling a complex corporate or consumer workflow, I don't stop at wireframes: I structure the information architecture into clean modular trees, simplify dense forms through visual chunking and inline validation, and validate keyboard ergonomics directly in code.

This dual approach bridges the traditional canyon between product management, design, and software engineering. Engineering teams receive battle-tested specs, clear acceptance criteria, and working reference implementations—cutting misinterpretations before a single sprint ticket is estimated.
