Google Calendar: Bringing the Selection Box to Schedule Changes

Drag to select, click to create: why moving four meetings in Google Calendar requires four separate drags, and how a standard selection box makes rescheduling effortless.

Application
Google Calendar (Web / Desktop)
Focus
Interaction Ergonomics
Role
Product & Interaction Designer
Core Hypothesis
Batch drag makes rescheduling 4x faster

The Annoyance: Why Is Moving Meetings So Tedious?

In almost every desktop tool—Figma, your computer desktop, spreadsheets, audio software—clicking and dragging on empty space draws a selection box (marquee) to grab multiple items.

Google Calendar does the exact opposite: clicking and dragging creates a brand new blank meeting.

So when your morning runs late and you need to push four back-to-back meetings back by 30 minutes, you have to drag them one by one. It’s slow, tedious, and easy to accidentally mess up planned break intervals or meeting lengths.

The Core Problem: Rescheduling a busy afternoon shouldn't take 8 precise mouse drags. It should take one fluid selection gesture.

3 Simple Changes to Make It Work

🖱️
Standard Gesture

Drag to Select

Clicking and dragging on empty grid space draws a selection box. All touching meetings get selected together.

⏱️
Spacing Preserved

Move as a Block

Dragging any selected meeting moves the entire group down, keeping the break intervals between them untouched.

✨
Quick Creation

Single Click to Create

A simple single click on an empty slot creates a new 30-minute meeting instantly, freeing the drag gesture for selection.

Why Hasn't Google Done This? (Design Trade-offs)

The Desktop vs. Touch Trade-off: On touchscreens, dragging is naturally reserved for scrolling. Google Calendar chose a single interaction model across devices, mapping drag to event creation. But on desktop computers with a mouse or trackpad, drawing a marquee box is the universal interaction pattern (Figma, desktop OS, spreadsheets). Distinguishing single-click creation from drag selection restores desktop muscle memory without adding visual clutter.
