Google Calendar: Rethinking the Interaction Model

In Google Calendar, every click-drag creates a new event. This concept flips the default: drag selects, click creates — the way desktop software works everywhere else.

Origins: Built from real-world frustration. Five years of using calendar grids daily, trying to drag-select multiple events to reschedule them, and repeatedly creating blank events by accident. This exact observation directly shaped the interaction model built into the Span Suite.

The Friction in the Grid

In Google Calendar's week view, click-dragging always creates a new event — it never selects existing ones. To select multiple events, there is no gesture at all. You are forced to click into and reschedule each block one by one.

This breaks a fundamental interaction pattern: in every desktop environment (file explorers, canvas tools, spreadsheets), click-drag represents marquee selection. Google Calendar's deviation causes persistent friction whenever a meeting slips or an entire afternoon shifts.

The Hypothesis: Desktop Standards Restored

If calendar interfaces adopted the desktop standard — drag to select, single-click to create inline — schedule reorganizations take seconds rather than minutes, with zero onboarding curve.

From Concept to Production in Span

Rather than keeping this as an abstract wireframe, I translated this exact principle into Span Plan: reducing multi-step dialog boxes to immediate, direct manipulations on time blocks.
