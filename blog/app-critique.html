Structured Critique of an App: Deconstructing Friction

Three friction points identified in daily mobile interfaces, with focused architectural solutions, core hypotheses, and measurable success metrics.

Category
UX Audit & Teardown
Framework
Friction / Hypothesis / Metric
Reading time
3 min

The Premise: Critique Is Engineering

Complaining about bad software is easy. Deconstructing why an interface fails under cognitive load—and proposing a deterministic solution—is engineering.

When users struggle with a flow, the root cause is almost never visual styling. It is a breakdown in the underlying mental model: the software assumes one sequence of intent, while the human in the real world is operating under competing constraints.

Point 01: The Hidden Destination Platform

Observation: In transit apps, connecting train platform numbers are nested two levels deep behind tap targets, forcing anxious passengers to reload screens while walking down stairs.
  • Friction: High motor load during moments of peak physical movement and poor cellular signal.
  • Hypothesis: Surfacing arrival and departure tracks automatically within 5 minutes of terminal approach reduces in-app navigation time to zero.
  • Measurable Metric: Drop in time-to-first-platform-view from 14s to < 1s via ambient lock-screen widgets.

Point 02: Modal Dialogs That Steal Working Memory

In many productivity tools, adding a subtask opens a centered modal that covers the existing list. The moment the dialog obscures the surrounding tasks, the user loses their frame of reference:

  • Friction: Context switching. The brain must hold the priority queue in memory while typing into a blank field.
  • Hypothesis: Inline, keyboard-driven creation directly beneath the target item maintains visual anchor points and preserves flow.
  • Measurable Metric: Task creation completion rate increases by 22%; cancellation rate drops to near zero.

Point 03: Passive Backlogs Without Capacity Checks

To-do lists that allow unlimited unchecked items without a temporal anchor create psychological debt. Users stop opening the tool once the backlog exceeds 30 tasks.

  • Friction: Infinite scroll paralysis. Without boundaries, all tasks appear equally urgent.
  • Hypothesis: Enforcing a structural cap (maximum 3 daily focal blocks with automated rollover) eliminates guilt and restores trust in the system.
  • Measurable Metric: Daily retention at Day 30 and average completion ratio of committed items.

Applied in Real Projects

These observations are not hypothetical exercises. Point 01 directly formed the basis of the SNCB Mobile UX concept, while Points 02 and 03 established the architectural foundation for the Span Suite.
