The Span Suite: Planning Without Overwhelm, Executing Without Clutter

Two interconnected web apps I designed and built to fix how I organize my day: one to plan 3–4 realistic daily blocks, and one to focus on a single task without seeing the rest of the list.

1. The Problem: The Endless List Trap

Most to-do apps make it too easy to add 50 tasks. But staring at a giant list every morning doesn't make you productive—it just makes you anxious.

Traditional To-Do Apps

The Infinite Inventory

Dumping dozens of items into a vertical checklist turns your tools into a source of guilt rather than clarity.

  • Morning Overwhelm: Waking up to 40 open tasks creates decision fatigue before you even open your laptop.
  • Hiding in Sub-Folders: Organizing everything into nested tags feels productive, but urgent tasks end up buried out of sight.
  • Overdue Shame: Yesterday's unfinished tasks turn into aggressive red badges that kill your motivation.
What I Built Instead

Protecting Real Calendar Time

I separated planning from doing: you choose your battles in the morning, and execute in peace during the day.

  • Daily Cap (Max 3–4 Blocks): You only schedule what can realistically fit in a day. The rest stays safely in the backlog.
  • Automatic Rollover: What you didn't finish today moves quietly to tomorrow. No red alarms, no shame.
  • Hidden Backlog during Sprints: When the 25-minute timer starts, the to-do list disappears. Only the active task is visible.

2. Principles: Simple Rules Behind the System

📦
From Supply Chain

Available-to-Promise

In warehousing, you don't promise a delivery without checking physical stock. Time works the same way: you can't commit to 8 tasks if you only have 2 free hours.

⚡
Ergonomics

100% Keyboard Flow

Reaching for the mouse every 20 minutes breaks concentration. Every action works with simple keys: Space to pause, Tab to edit, Enter to finish.

📴
Real Life

Offline by Default

When you're on a train going through a tunnel, the app shouldn't freeze. Changes save locally in 0ms, and sync quietly to the cloud when internet returns.

7. Real-World Dogfooding Log: What I Learned Using My Own Apps

The best design decisions came from using both apps every single day to run my own work:

Week 1 · The 8:00 AM Guilt Checkmarks User Psychology

What I noticed: Seeing red overdue marks from yesterday first thing in the morning made me feel like I was already behind schedule before even starting.
Design change: I removed all overdue penalties. Unfinished tasks now roll over automatically into today's list without red marks or guilt.

Week 3 · Why I Stopped Touching the Mouse Micro-Interaction

What I noticed: Having to grab the trackpad to start or pause the timer broke my concentration while writing or coding.
Design change: I mapped every key action to the keyboard: Space to start/pause, Tab to edit, Enter to mark done. You never have to leave the home row.

Week 6 · Train Tunnels & Spotty 4G Technical Resilience

What I noticed: Losing connection on train commutes caused loading spinners to freeze the app when completing tasks.
Design change: Made the app save to local browser storage (IndexedDB) first. UI updates in 0ms; syncing to Supabase happens quietly in the background when signal returns.

9. Real-World Engineering Trade-offs

Challenge The Real Problem How I Solved It
Midnight Rollover Unfinished tasks piled up as scary red overdue items, creating fatigue. Built an automatic rollover so tasks gently move to the next day with zero penalty.
Spotty train Wi-Fi Loading spinners froze the app in tunnels when finishing tasks. Saved everything to local storage first (0ms latency), syncing to cloud in the background.
Mobile Load Time Heavy libraries made the app feel sluggish on older phones. Kept the bundle lightweight so the PWA opens in under 200ms on any mobile browser.
